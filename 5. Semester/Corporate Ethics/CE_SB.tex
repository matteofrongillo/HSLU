\subsubsection{Morality}
Norms, values, and beliefs in social processes, which define right
and wrong for individuals and communities.

\subsubsection{Ethics}
Study of morality that focuses on explaining rules and principles that
determine right or wrong.

\subsubsection{Ethical theory}
Rules and principles are called ethical theories.

\subsection{Why is business corporate ethics important}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Power/influence of business in society (eg. lobbies, multinationals, banks)
    \item Potential to provide major contribution to society $\to$ provide employment, tax payments, solve social problems (eg. health)
    \item Malpractice can inflict enormous harm to society or environment (eg. accidents)
    \item Increasing demand from skateholders/society to act ethically
    \item Lack of business ethics education or training
    \item Businesses face trust decifit
    \item Growth of business ethics 'industry' (consultants, ethical products/services)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Large vs small companies}

        \item Lack of time and resources
        \item Less responsabilites to skateholders
    \end{itemize}
    \item Large companies have much more formalized approach to manage BE
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Globalization}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Globalization is a process which diminishes the necessity of a common and shared territorial basis for social, economic, and political activities, processes, and relations (``deterritorialization'')
    \item Globalization makes real-time social, political, economic, and cultural exchanges possible between people and organizations without the need for physical contact
\end{itemize}

Globalization was supported by two main developments:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Technology (communication, transportation, digital financial streams)
    \item Policy (national territorial borders have been eroded)
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Effects}
\begin{itemize}
    \item European financial crisis $\to$ events, which have local roots, have effects on seemingly disconnected places
    \item Global climate change $\to$ Local causes leads to global problems and global solutions (eg. Kyoto Protocol)
    \item Acts of Terror $\to$ events, people or ideas from faraway places can have effects on people on unconnected locations/situations (eg. security checks in airports)
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Ethics in the context of globalization}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Topics like fair trade, food prices, financialstability have set ethical spotlight on globalization process
    \item MNCs (Multi-National Corporations) are at the center of public's criticism on globalization\\$\to$ accused to exploit workers, cause environmental destruction,abuse their economic power
    \item Globalization is the most demanding area for corporation to define and legitimate their ``right and wrong'' behavior
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Concepts distinct from globalization}
\subsubsection{Internationalization}
Internationalization is the expansion of activities across national borders while those activities remain anchored in particular countries.

\subsubsection{Westernization}
Westernization is the spread or adoption of Western cultural practices, values, and institutions.

\subsubsection{Homogenisation}
Homogenisation is the process by which cultures, products, or practices become more similar to one another.

\subsection{Relevance of globalization for business ethics}
\subsubsection{Cultural issues}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Corporations increasingly engage in overseas markets
    \item Confronted with new, diverse or even contradictory ethical demands
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Legal issues}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Linked to relationship between ethics and law
    \item Transactions lose connection to certain territorial entity
    \item Escape legal control of national governments
    \item Globalization increases demand for business ethics (beyond control of national governments)
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Accountability issues}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Some MNCs are as economically powerful as entire countries
    \item How to control MNCs, which have higher revenues than GDPs of entire countries?
    \item Demand for corporate accountability
\end{itemize}

\section{Framing business ethics}
\subsection{Stock corporation}
A corporation is defined in terms of legal status and the ownership
of assets:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Legally corporations are independents from those who manage, work, invest in them or receive products or services from them
    \item Corporation have perpetual succession
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Corporation assets owners}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A corporation owns its own assets (eg. factories, equipment)
    \item Shareholders only own share, which entitles them from dividends and some decisions
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Implications of corporation definition for determining responsibilities}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Corporations are regarded as ``artificial persons'' in the eyes of the law\\$\to$ have certain rights and (legal) responsibilities in society
    \item Corporations are ``owned'' by shareholders, but exists independently of them\\$\to$ shareholders are not responbile for debts or damages
    \item Managers and directors have responsibility to protect investment of shareholders
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Can a corporation have social responsibility?}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Only human beings have a moral responsibility for their actions
    \item It is managers' responsibility to act solely in the interests of shareholders (profit oriented)
    \item Social issues and problems are the proper province of the state rather than corporate managers\\$\to$ corporations should not decide what is in society's best interest
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{``The social responsibility of business is to increase its profits''}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Corporations can decide/act independent of their members\\
        $\to$ Corporate internal decision structure based on predetermined goals
    \item All corporations have a set of beliefs and values (organizational culture)
    \begin{itemize}[label=$\to$]
        \item Influence on individual's ethical decision-making and behavior
        \item Corporations present and interact with stakeholders as if they were distinct persons
    \end{itemize}
    \item Corporations have some level of moral responsibility that is more than the responsibility of the individuals constituing the corporation
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why do corporations have and take on social responsibilities?}
\subsubsection{Internal business reasons (``enlightened self-interest'')}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Extra and/or satisfied customers/employees $\to$ enhances long-term revenues
    \item Cost reduction due to increased sustainability $\to$ saving energy, waste reduction, less inefficiencies
    \item Manage risks and uncertainty
    \begin{itemize}[label=$\to$]
        \item Committing to social actions/programmes may forestall legislation
        \item Prevent penalties
    \end{itemize}
    \item Long-term investment which benefits corporation $\to$ Maintaining consent of local communities, employees, governments
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Moral reasons}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Corporations cause social problems (externalities)\\
        $\to$ Moral responsibility to deal with them (eg. pollution, resource depletion, community problems)
    \item Corporations should use their power responsibly
    \item Corporations rely on the contribution of a wide set of stakeholders in society, not just shareholders\\
        $\to$ Have a duty to also take into account the interests of stakeholders
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stakeholder theory of the firm}
\begin{itemize}
    \item CSR approach focuses on types of responsibilities of corporations towards society and specific stakeholders
    \item A stakeholder of an organization is any group or individual who can affect or is affected by the achievement of the organization's objectives (Freeman)
    \item `Affects' and `affected by' can be explained by two principles
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Principle of corporate rights $\to$ Corporation has obligation not to violate the rights of others
        \item Principle of corporate effect $\to$ Companies responsible for the effects of their actions on others
    \end{itemize}
    \item Stakeholders vary from company to company and for the same company in different situations
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Stakeholder thinking in an international context}
International examples of stakeholder integration
\begin{itemize}
    \item German supervisory boards of corporations must include employee representatives
    \item ``Scandinavian Cooperative Advantage''\\
    $\to$ Participative management strategies (consensus building, stakeholder involvement)
    \item Keiretsu system in Japan (Chaebol in Korea)\\
    $\to$ Network of banks, manufacturers, suppliers and service providers\\
    $\to$ Member companies own small portions of shares in each other's companies
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Different forms of Stakeholder theory of the firm}
\pph{Normative stakeholder theory}
Attemps to provide a reason why corporations should take into account
stakeholders interests

\pph{Descriptive stakeholder theory}
Attemps to ascertain whether (and how) corporations actually do take
into account stakeholders interests

\pph{Instrumental stakeholder theory}
Attempts to answer whether it is beneficial for corporations to take
into account stakeholders interets
